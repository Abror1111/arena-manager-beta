# ARENA 1.16 — map readability, collision and match presentation

Map scenes keep the existing genuine CS2 GLBs and texture hashes. The viewer uses warmer, lower-exposure lighting with directional shadows, framed to each map. Phones cache static world shadows; battery mode disables them. Agent contact shadows remain cheap. A/B overview markers clarify the target sites. Themed exterior terraces, retaining edges and sparse vegetation/buildings close exposed backs outside the playable bounds; source floors, navigation coordinates and map geometry remain unchanged. Ancient Night still uses a simplified visual scene.

Existing source collision triangles are indexed in a two-map bounded spatial grid before play begins. Gun sight lines use these triangles after the nav visibility filter, preventing shots through opaque walls. Grenades integrate gravity in bounded steps, collide with real triangles, reflect from walls/floors and retain a short trail. Molotovs bounce from walls and break on floor contact; their fire is grounded against collision or nav surfaces. Legacy snapshots with old grenade arcs still resume. The 2D-only failure path has a nav/block fallback rather than pretending to have loaded the full triangle mesh.

Agents stop moving after a round. Nav seam recovery explicitly walks through the bridge endpoint instead of cutting across a source gap. Teammates holding a choke give way when a blocked ally needs to pass; local rerouting avoids the obstruction. Nothing pushes or teleports a player through geometry. The shorter opening preparation and gradual control advance reduce idle time. Burst damage, reaction, recoil and brief cover retreats produce longer exchanges while keeping weapon identities distinct.

A losing team changes its opening variant (contact, split, fast or late); balanced attacks also reconsider the site. Known own-team casualties become soft route penalties. Plans never read hidden enemies' current locations. Experienced players react and shoot more effectively, but these remain seeded reactive rules rather than human-level AI. Different plans do not guarantee a win or eliminate all mistakes.

C4 aftermath stays visible for five simulation seconds at normal playback speed even when the match uses fast playback. The result panel and next-round control wait for the aftermath. Auto camera follows the explosion; props continue their physical motion while agents stop. Pausing also pauses the scene.

The transfer market includes inexpensive rookies and experienced players, with different skills, match history, price and potential. Experience modifies effective lineup strength and in-match reaction/aim. Training respects a recruit's potential; buy/sell values cannot generate a transfer arbitrage. Club credits and match equipment money remain separate.

Audio is an ORIGINAL PROCEDURAL approximation, not recordings from CS2. Weapon-family shots, footsteps, reloads, bounces, grenade/flash/fire/smoke/C4 effects and round signals use bounded Web Audio voices and a limiter. No microphone or remote audio request is used. The user-provided map/agent/VFX archives contain no sound recordings. The accompanying read-only export tool can extract genuine sounds from an installed CS2 for later adaptation. No game assets are downloaded by that tool; it downloads only the official pinned Source2Viewer CLI.

Career schema 2, the saved-club key and Android upgrade signer remain compatible. Old saved rounds gain new planning at the next round. Physical Android GPU performance must still be assessed on a real device; browser tests use Chromium software rendering.
